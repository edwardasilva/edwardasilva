The CSCI 406 film festival asked for a 3 to 6 minute film on an algorithm or method from class. The film had to show a real-world use and an ethical problem it raises. My team picked graph modeling in social media recommendations and animated the film in Manim, a Python library for math animation.

The film starts with a road map, where intersections are nodes and roads are weighted edges. The scene defines the roads as weighted pairs:

```python title="Scenes/Intro/__init__.py"
roads = [
    ("A", "B", "4"),
    ("B", "C", "3"),
    ("B", "D", "6"),
    ("C", "E", "2"),
    ("C", "D", "5"),
    ("E", "F", "4"),
    ("C", "F", "7"),
    ("D", "G", "3"),
    ("F", "G", "2"),
]
```

The film then makes each user a node, which lets a platform find similar users and recommend what they engage with. Platforms earn money from engagement, so the film follows what happens when a graph favors whatever holds attention. It names five problems:

- Amplifying emotionally charged content
- Spreading misinformation
- Manipulating attention through triggers such as fear and outrage
- Building addictive usage patterns
- Reinforcing echo chambers

The film is five Manim scenes, rendered one at a time and joined at the end. They cover an opening hook, the recommendation network, what a graph is, recommending from similar users, and the retention objective.
